# M3.10 – My game! 🚀

## 🎯 The big mission

Now make **your own game**.

You may reuse everything you have built. You decide what the game becomes.

When you finish, you should be able to say:

**I made my own game!**

## 💡 Choose an idea

Write one sentence:

**In my game, the player ____________________.**

Then decide: What do you control? What is the goal? What is dangerous? How do you score? Can you win or lose?

A small game that works is better than a huge game that never gets finished.

## 🧱 Build with checkpoints

### 1 – I can move

Get the player on screen and make the controls work. Test.

### 2 – I can do something

Add something to collect, catch, or reach. Test again.

### 3 – The game remembers

Add score, lives, or another value.

### 4 – It gets exciting

Add the monster or another challenge.

### 5 – The game has an ending

Decide what happens when the player wins or loses.

### 6 – Make it yours

Give it a name, colours, and at least one rule you chose.

## 🧰 Help cards

Look at earlier missions or `examples/m3-mini-game.py` whenever you need a reminder. Looking things up is not cheating — programmers do it all the time.

Useful tools include `goto()`, `xcor()`, `ycor()`, `distance()`, `random.randint()`, and `ontimer()`.

## 🧪 Test like a game maker

Try strange things too: hold a key, run into an edge, get caught several times, try to win, and try to lose.

Do the rules behave the way you expected?

## 👥 Find a playtester

Let someone else play without explaining every key. Ask what they thought the goal was, what was fun, what was confusing, and what they would change.

Choose **one** improvement and make it.

## 🐞 When something goes wrong

Ask what you changed last, which small part is failing, whether `print()` can reveal a useful value, and whether you can test just that part.

A bug is something to investigate.

## 🏁 Finished?

Your game is ready when:

- [ ] it starts
- [ ] the player can control something
- [ ] there is a goal
- [ ] at least one game rule works
- [ ] the player can tell what is happening
- [ ] winning/losing has been tested where your game uses it
- [ ] another person has tried it
- [ ] you improved at least one thing after testing
- [ ] the game has a name
- [ ] you can show one piece of code and explain what it does

It does not need to be perfect. It needs to be **yours**.

## 🎤 Show your game

Tell someone its name, what the player does, what was most fun to build, one bug you found, and show one piece of code you understand well.

## 🧠 Look what you made

You started with small Python programs. Now you have used variables, `if`, loops, functions, randomness, coordinates, key presses, and graphics to build a game that reacts to a player.

**You made your own game.** 🎉
